I want to try something new to use AI productively: I have a bucketful of ideas that never make it into papers and I want to change that.

Here's the issue: I'm not great at converting. I read and write too slowly, I get distracted, I lack patience. But I do have above average intuition and common sense that lead to lots of paper ideas. Most, however, never go anywhere. I'll now change that.

My interest is broadly in applied theory, crossing over also into light empirics. The key skill is the ability to identify interesting questions. Some people make their careers by patching holes in the literature, others, like me, by hopping around areas, driven by curiosity. But the question is what matters.

The execution (i.e., the answering) of questions takes time and patience. And that's where AI comes in: Our tools in economics aren't set, but you can get very far using what we already have. AI can do much of the heavy lifting, at least that's what I think, and I want to try.

Over 20 years in academic finance, I've generated far more ideas than I could ever write up. Some turned into papers I'm proud of — finding conditions for rational, information-driven herding, developing ways to test for the causal effects of high frequency traders and maker-taker fees, or, more recently, showing how frontrunning creates unlimited loss potential in limit order markets and AMMs, modeling utility tokens and their optimal design, or identifying the fundamental ownership problem for tokenized stocks in DeFi. Of course, many of these papers and ideas were developed with my brilliant co-authors.

But for every paper I finished, several more died on the vine. Studies on cross-border trading and payment-for-order-flow. The first study of on-exchange dark trading. Work connecting AMM pricing to traditional limit order books in ways that give theoretical foundations to a large part of empirical market microstructure. These aren't minor ideas — they address real problems in how our markets work. I just couldn't write fast enough.

But now there's AI.

It works with the latest models and tools, so I can take an idea and produce a serious draft in hours instead of weeks.

My process: I spend the week reading, listening to podcasts, talking with people, arguing with my kids, working things through with Katya. Then on the odd Saturday morning, I sit down with an AI and shape an idea into a paper.

So I'm starting a blog. Each post will lay out an idea and include an AI-assisted paper — produced with whichever model does the best job. I'll post them as-is. These are not polished journal submissions. They are idea papers: fast, hopefully substantive, and transparent about how they were made.

Some of these ideas touch market structure and regulation. Some touch DeFi and tokenization. Some touch the intersection of the two, which is where I think the most important questions are right now. My hope is that they're useful: to academia in starting a discussion about an important topic, to policymakers thinking about these problems, and to practitioners trying to understand where markets are heading.

What happens next is open. I may develop some into full papers. I may not. I'm genuinely open to co-authorship — if something here excites you, reach out.

Why am I doing this? Because I like ideas. I like learning. I like telling people what I've learned. And some of these ideas are too valuable to leave on the shelf.

The first post is up now. Let me know what you think.
